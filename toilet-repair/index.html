Overflowing toilet: stop the water first

An overflowing toilet is an emergency that can ruin a floor in minutes, and our 24/7 line takes that call. Reach behind the toilet and turn the shutoff valve clockwise until it stops. If there is no valve or it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.

Do not flush again and do not plunge a toilet that is still filling. Put towels around the base and call us if the water does not drain.

Symptoms.

Toilets that will not flush or keep running

Most toilet problems come from a handful of parts in the tank.

Running or hissing

A worn flapper or fill valve is letting water leak into the bowl.

Weak flush

Clogged rim jets, a low water level or a partly blocked trap.

Wobbles or leaks at the base

A failed wax ring or loose bolts, which can rot the floor if ignored.

Tank sweats or leaks

Condensation or a failed tank-to-bowl gasket.

Parts.

Fill valves, flappers and the parts that wear out

The fill valve controls how much water enters the tank, and the flapper seals the tank to the bowl. After years of use, both can fail, and a running toilet can waste a lot of water.

We replace them with parts that match your toilet. A dye test with a few drops of food coloring in the tank shows whether the flapper is leaking. If the color shows in the bowl without flushing, the seal has failed.

Clogs.

Clogs and the stubborn blockage

A toilet clog that a plunger cannot clear needs a closet auger or a drain cable. If several fixtures back up together, the problem is in the main line, not the toilet.

If we have to pull the toilet to clear it, we reset it with a new wax ring. Frequent toilet clogs can point to a deeper problem, and our drain cleaning page explains how we check the line.

Decision.

Repair or replace the toilet

A toilet with a cracked bowl, a leak you cannot trace or an old design that needs repeated fixes is worth replacing. A newer toilet uses less water and often flushes better.

We help you choose a height, shape and flush rating that fits the bathroom. For a larger bathroom project, our plumbing installation service covers the rest of the fixtures.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) limits new toilets to 1.6 gallons per flush, so a replacement may flush with less water than an old one. We check the flush rating on the tank when we match a replacement.

Repair

  • Lowest cost for a single worn part.
  • Quick, often one visit.

Replace

Common Questions.

Toilet repair questions in Windham

Do you fix toilet emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Shut the valve behind the toilet first.

Why does my toilet keep running?

A worn flapper or fill valve is the common cause. A dye test tells you which, and the repair is often quick.

Why is my toilet leaking at the base?

A failed wax ring or loose bolts. Do not ignore it, because it can rot the subfloor.

How do I know if the clog is in the toilet or the main line?

If only the toilet is affected, it is a local clog. If a tub or sink also gurgles or backs up, the main line is blocked.

Can you install a new toilet?

Yes. We remove the old one, check the flange, set the new toilet with a new wax ring and test for leaks.

Can a toilet repair wait until morning?

A slow leak or a running toilet can often wait. An overflow cannot. Close the shutoff valve and call.

How much water does a running toilet waste?

It can waste many gallons a day and raise your bill. A repair pays for itself quickly.
