A burst or failed tank is an emergency replacement

When a tank splits you have two jobs. Stop the water and power first, then get a replacement in. Our 24/7 line takes that call at any hour, and we can often install the same day.

Switch off the breaker or gas control, close the cold-water valve so the tank stops refilling and the leak slows. If water is reaching the floor drain, leave it be. If it is pooling, move stored items off the floor.

Decision.

When replacing beats repairing

Some failures are cheap to fix and some are not worth fixing. A tank that leaks from the seam is finished. So is a tank with a corroded base, a cracked flue baffle or repeated element burnouts.

Age matters too. If the heater is well past ten years and needs a major part, the repair money is better spent on a new tank with a fresh warranty. We lay out both options so the choice is yours.

Sizing.

Choosing size and fuel for your household

The right tank depends on how many people shower, how many baths you fill and whether the dishwasher and laundry run at the same time. A family of five on a 30-gallon tank will notice. A couple in a small home may be paying to heat water they never use.

Fuel is usually fixed by what the house already has. Switching from electric to gas or oil adds venting and piping work, so we price that separately and only when it makes sense.

The job.

What happens on replacement day

We shut off and drain the old heater, disconnect the supply and gas or electric lines and carry the old tank out. Then we set the new unit in a drain pan, connect new flexible lines or copper, and pipe a code-compliant relief valve discharge.

Gas units get a fresh venting check and a leak test at every joint. Electric units get tested for correct voltage and thermostat settings. We fill the tank, purge air from the lines and run hot water at several fixtures before we leave.

Code.

Permits, code and the parts people forget

Connecticut adopts the International Plumbing Code (IPC) in its State Building Code, and water heater work follows it. That means a relief valve with a proper discharge pipe, shutoff valves and, in many homes, an expansion tank. Local permit rules vary by town, so we ask about your address when we schedule.

We also look at the venting, the gas line and the drain pan under the unit. Fixing these at the same visit costs less than coming back, and it keeps the install on the right side of an inspection.

Common Questions.

Replacement questions we hear in Windham

Do you replace water heaters in emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. If the tank is leaking, say so on the call so we can walk you through shutting it down.

How long does a water heater replacement take?

Most standard tank swaps take a few hours. Moving from one fuel to another, or fixing old venting, takes longer. We give you a realistic window when we schedule.

Do you remove the old water heater?

Yes. We drain it, disconnect it and take it with us as part of the job, so you are not left with a dead tank in the basement.

Can I switch from electric to gas, or the other way?

Sometimes. It depends on gas service, venting and electrical capacity at your address. We check those on the call or on site and price the extra work separately.

Will my homeowner insurance pay for a failed tank?

It depends on your policy. Sudden failures and the damage they cause are sometimes covered, while age and wear usually are not. Ask your insurer and keep photos of the failure.

Do I need a permit for a new water heater?

Many towns do require one for replacements. We ask about your address when we book and tell you what applies before the work starts.

What size tank should I buy for a family of four?

A 40 to 50 gallon tank is a common fit for four people with two baths. Hard-use homes with soaking tubs may want to go larger. We can size it on the call.
