Overview.

What we install

This page covers planned plumbing work for remodels, additions and new homes. If something is broken now, our other service pages handle that, and this one is for projects.

We install sinks, faucets, toilets, showers, tubs, laundry hookups, water heater connections and the supply and drain lines that go with them.

Remodels.

Bathroom and kitchen remodels

A remodel is the best time to fix hidden plumbing. We open the walls, check the supply lines and drains, replace old valves and set the rough-in for new fixtures before the finish work goes in.

We coordinate with your contractor on timing, such as rough-in before drywall and trim after tile. A missed step means opening finished walls, so we plan the order of work.

Fixtures.

New fixtures and appliance hookups

Dishwashers, ice makers, washers and utility sinks all need correct supply and drain connections. We install shutoffs, braided lines and proper drains so these appliances do not become the next leak.

For fixtures like a new toilet or faucet, see our toilet service and faucet page. For a disposal connection, our disposal page explains the details.

Gas.

Gas lines and safety

Gas appliance connections are safety work. If you smell gas, leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber. Do not turn any switches or use your phone inside.

For planned gas work, such as a range, dryer or heater connection, we check the line size, test connections and follow local code. Some gas work requires a permit, and we tell you when it does.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and the 2020 National Electrical Code (NEC), both adopted through the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, set the rules for fixtures, venting and power connections. Where a permit is required, we say so up front.

Treatment.

Water softeners and filtration

Softeners and filters are common additions, especially on homes with well water. We install the plumbing for a unit you have chosen, including bypass valves, drain lines and the connection to the supply.

The right unit depends on a water test, and we recommend a certified lab test before you buy. We do not recommend a system without knowing what is in the water.

New builds.

New construction and additions

We rough in supply and drain lines for new homes and additions, coordinate with inspections and finish fixtures once walls and floors are in. A clear layout from the start prevents rework.

Tell us your plans, fixture counts and timelines. We can price rough-in and finish separately if you prefer.

Common Questions.

Plumbing installation questions

Do you work with my contractor?

Yes. We coordinate rough-in and finish with general contractors and tell them when we need access.

Do you install gas lines?

We handle gas appliance connections and related piping where local rules allow. Anything near a leak means evacuating and calling the utility first.

Can you install a water softener I bought?

Yes. We connect the supply, bypass and drain. We recommend a lab water test first.

Do I need a permit for a remodel?

Often yes. We tell you what applies once we know your address and scope.

Can you move a sink or toilet to another wall?

Often yes, but drains need slope and venting. We review the layout and tell you what it takes.

How far ahead should I book?

As early as your plans allow, so the work fits your contractor schedule.
