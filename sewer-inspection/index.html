Overview.

What a sewer camera inspection is

A sewer camera inspection is a look inside the line from the house toward the street using a small waterproof camera on a flexible rod. The picture shows on a monitor in real time, and we record it so you can review it later.

It is a planned service, not an emergency one. If your drain is backing up now, call our sewer line repair line instead and we will put cleaning and diagnosis together.

Reasons.

When an inspection is worth booking

An inspection pays for itself in situations where the cost of not knowing is high.

Before buying a home

A line problem can cost thousands to fix, and you can ask the seller to address it before closing.

Repeat clogs

If the same drain keeps clogging, a camera finds the cause instead of treating the symptom.

Before a renovation

New bathrooms and kitchens tie into old lines. We check they can handle it.

Mature trees near the line

Roots can enter joints, and a camera shows how far the problem has gone.

Findings.

What we look for on camera

We watch for cracks, offset joints, bellies where water collects, root intrusion, scale, collapsed sections and failed repairs. We note the pipe material and the distance from the entry point so you have a record of where each issue sits.

A locator on the camera head lets us mark the surface above anything that needs work. That removes guesswork if you decide to dig and gives any contractor a clear target.

We judge slope, joints and cleanout access against what the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) expects of a sewer, and we note anything that falls short. That gives a buyer or owner a plain reference instead of an opinion.

Results.

What you receive after an inspection

You get a plain description of the line condition, a list of any problems with their locations and a copy of the footage when you ask for it. We also tell you what needs attention now and what can wait.

We do not push repairs. If the line is sound we say so, and if it needs work we explain the options. For common cleaning follow-ups, our drain cleaning service and hydro jetting pages explain the next steps.

Common Questions.

Sewer camera inspection questions

How long does a sewer camera inspection take?

Most take under an hour, though long lines or those that need cleaning first can take longer. We tell you what to expect when we schedule.

Can you inspect the line without a cleanout?

Often yes. We can reach the line through a toilet or other opening, though a cleanout is easier and we can talk about adding one.

Will I get a copy of the video?

Yes, if you ask for it. We can also describe the findings in writing.

Do you do inspections for home buyers?

Yes. Book as early as you can during the inspection period so the results come back in time.

What if the camera cannot pass through the line?

That usually means a blockage or collapse. We tell you what we found, and if cleaning will help, we explain that option.

Is a camera inspection the same as a plumbing inspection?

No. A camera inspection looks only at the drain and sewer line. A full plumbing inspection covers supply lines, fixtures and the water heater.
