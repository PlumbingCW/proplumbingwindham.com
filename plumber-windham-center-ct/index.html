The area.

The village around Windham Green

Windham Center is the original settlement of the town. It began in the 1690s as Hither Place and was the county seat until 1819. For about 125 years it was the most densely settled part of Windham, before Willimantic grew past it.

Today it is a National Register historic district of about 205 acres centered on the green. Four roads lead out from it, Scotland Road, Windham Center Road, Plains Road and North Road. The listing recorded 78 buildings, with houses, a church, a post office and a former bank among them.

Low-density housing

The town plan describes the wider area as mostly low-density residential and farm land.

Room left to grow

The plan notes a great deal of undeveloped land in the area.

A rural feel

The National Register record calls the village essentially rural.

Service varies by road

The plan does not map water and sewer here, so confirm yours.

Water and waste.

Wells, septic and public water

The plan does not state which parts of Windham Center are on public water or sewer. A home on a municipal main is billed for water. A home without one usually has a private well with a pump and pressure tank, and a septic system for waste. Which you have is a fact about your address, not the village.

Connecticut Department of Public Health offers private well guidance, including advice on water testing. We handle the plumbing side of a well system, such as the lines, fittings and fixtures, and we tell you when a problem belongs with a well or pump specialist.

Common calls.

Pressure, pumps and slow fixtures

A pump that cycles on and off often, sputtering faucets and a pressure gauge that swings widely can all point to the pressure tank or pump. A sudden drop at every fixture can also mean a leak between the well and the house. We check the simple items first.

If the leak is outside, our water service line repair page describes how a buried supply line is found and fixed. For a hidden leak inside, leak detection pinpoints it with minimal opening.

Older homes.

Older houses around the green

Buildings here include Greek Revival, Colonial and Italianate styles, and the former bank on the green dates to 1832. Age alone does not tell us what pipe is inside. Many have been updated in stages, so we look at the actual lines before we suggest a repair.

Water heaters in houses like these may sit in crawl spaces or small utility closets. Our water heater replacement page explains how we plan a swap when space is tight.

Common Questions.

Windham Center plumbing questions

Do you serve Windham Center ZIP 06280?

Yes. Give us your road name when you call and we plan the visit from there.

How do I know if I am on a well or public water?

Check whether you receive a water bill. If you do not, you likely have a well. Your town or our visit can confirm it.

Can you help with a well pump or pressure tank?

We handle the plumbing around them, such as lines and fittings. When the well or pump itself is the failed part, we say so and send you to a specialist for that piece.

My house is in the historic district. Does that change your work?

Interior plumbing work is normal for us. If a repair would change the outside of the house, we tell you first so you can check with the town.

Where does Windham Center end and North Windham begin?

The town plan calls the line not particularly clear. Your ZIP and road name are enough for us.
