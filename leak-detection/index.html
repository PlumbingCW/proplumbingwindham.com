Start here.

Active leak or wet ceiling right now

Water coming through a ceiling or wall is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is open for it. Shut off the main water valve, switch off the power to the wet area at the breaker and put a bucket under any drip.

Do not poke at a bulging ceiling with your finger. If it is sagging, a small hole at the lowest point can release the water in a controlled way, and we can tell you whether to do that or wait for us.

Above you.

Ceiling, wall and attic leaks

Water from above is usually a supply line, a drain or a bathroom fixture upstairs. It can also be a roof leak that looks like plumbing. We check the fixture above, the supply lines and the drain with moisture meters and thermal imaging.

If the source is not plumbing, we tell you so and you can call a roofer. That saves you paying for the wrong repair.

High bill.

Water meter still running or a high bill

A water bill that jumps with no change in how you live is the leak signal most people notice first. Turn off every fixture, appliance and the ice maker, then watch the leak indicator on your meter. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere.

We then isolate the house using the main valve to learn whether the leak is inside or outside, and shut sections off one at a time. That narrows the search to a few pipes instead of the whole house.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) requires new water supply piping to be pressure tested before it is covered. We use the same principle on existing lines, isolating each branch and watching the gauge.

Below.

Under the house, in the crawlspace and under the slab

Leaks in crawlspaces and under floors show up as damp insulation, musty smells, mold or warm spots in a floor. Radiant heat and hot-water lines under a slab can leak without any wet floor at all.

We use pressure tests on each line and a listening device that hears the leak against the pipe. Thermal imaging finds the warm path of a hot-water leak. Slab leaks are not common in basement homes but can occur in additions and slab-on-grade rooms, and we tell you if your home fits that pattern.

Outside.

Underground, yard and irrigation leaks

A leak in the water line between the street and the house shows up as a wet spot that does not dry, low pressure or a meter that moves with the house shut off. Irrigation systems add their own leaks, and a sprinkler head or valve can be the culprit.

We trace the line with a locator and listening gear before any digging. If it is the service line, our water service line repair page explains the repair options. A pool or spa leak is a separate job, and we tell you if it is beyond what we do.

Local.

Leaks in Windham homes and buildings

Old plumbing in Willimantic mill-era houses and multi-family buildings has joints that have been repaired many times, and some supply lines run inside plaster walls. A slow leak can go unseen for months, so we ask about the age of the home and any past repairs.

If you are in Mansfield or Coventry on a private well, a leak may show as a pump that cycles too often rather than a high bill.

Common Questions.

Leak detection questions in Windham

Do you find leaks in emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Shut off the main water valve first if water is actively flowing.

How do you find a leak without opening walls?

We use pressure tests, acoustic listening gear, moisture meters and thermal imaging to narrow the search to a small area first.

Why is my water bill so high?

A hidden leak is one cause, along with running toilets, outdoor taps and irrigation. A meter test helps tell which.

Can a leak be under my slab?

It can, particularly in slab-on-grade additions. Most homes here have basements, and we confirm your layout before we look.

How do I know if a stain on my ceiling is a plumbing leak?

Check whether it grows after using a fixture above. If it grows only after rain, it may be the roof.

Do you detect leaks in irrigation systems?

Yes. We check the lines, valves and heads, and we tell you what we find.

Will my insurance cover leak detection?

It sometimes does when the leak caused a covered loss. Ask your insurer and keep the written findings.

Do you handle commercial leak detection?

Yes. Commercial leak work is quoted after we see the building, and we plan around business hours where we can.
