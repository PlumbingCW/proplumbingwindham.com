Neighborhoods.

Storrs, Mansfield Center and the streets around campus

Mansfield is a town of two personalities. Around Storrs and the University of Connecticut campus, homes are packed close with rental houses, duplexes and apartment buildings. Out in Mansfield Center and along the quieter roads, houses sit on bigger lots and many are older farmhouses and capes.

That mix decides which plumbing problems we see most. We ask at the start whether you are in a rental, an owner-occupied house or a small business, because the answer changes who authorizes work and what parts we bring.

Calls.

What Mansfield calls us about most

Drains lead the list in the student-rental areas. Toilets get clogged by wipes and paper products, kitchen lines collect grease and shared building lines back up. When one fixture backs up, we ask whether neighbors on the same line are affected, because that points to the main.

Water heaters are the next most common call. A tank that has run in a rental for years can fail with no warning and leave a house of tenants without hot water. See our drain cleaning page and water heater replacement page for how we handle both.

Before we arrive.

If a pipe bursts or a leak starts in Mansfield

A burst pipe or active leak is the call we move on first. These are the steps to take while we come to you. Each one limits the water damage before we arrive.

Shut off the main

Find the main water valve near the basement wall or meter and close it fully. If you are in a rental, tell your landlord and call us.

Kill power near water

Turn off breakers for wet rooms. Keep out of standing water around outlets or appliances.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the wet floor. Bring pets and children to a dry room.

Rentals.

Rentals, landlords and student housing

Landlords and tenants both need a clear record of what went wrong. We document what we find and write it in plain terms. If the fault is tenant misuse, such as a wipe clog, we say that. If the fault is old pipe or a worn heater, we say that too.

Turnover periods in late summer and spring bring a rush of calls. Landlords who book fixture checks before move-in save the cost of emergency visits later. Our toilet repair page and leak detection page cover the usual repeat issues.

Rural homes.

Wells, septic and the health district

Homes outside the core may use a private well and a septic system. Well water pressure, pump cycling and slow drains can point to the system rather than the house plumbing. Septic design and permits go through the local health district, which for Mansfield is the Eastern Highlands Health District.

We handle the plumbing side and tell you when a problem belongs to the tank, the field or the well. If you are not sure which you have, we can usually tell from the meter, the pressure tank and the cleanout. See our Coventry page for a similar setup nearby.

According to the Connecticut Department of Public Health, private well owners should test basic water quality indicators every year and retest after work on the pump or pipes. If your tap water changes after a plumbing repair, tell us and consider a test.
