No heat and a boiler that will not fire

A boiler that stops in winter is an emergency, and our 24/7 line is staffed for that call. While you wait, check that the thermostat is calling for heat, the switch on the boiler is on and the water pressure gauge is in the normal range.

Do not keep resetting a boiler that locks out repeatedly. If you smell gas or see water on the floor around the boiler, leave the area and call the gas utility or 911. If it is water on the floor, switch off power to the boiler at the breaker.

Common faults.

What a boiler call usually turns out to be

The cause of a no-heat call is rarely mysterious once we are on site. These are the most frequent.

No ignition

A dirty flame sensor, failed igniter or a tripped safety limit stops the burner.

Heat in some rooms only

A stuck zone valve, trapped air or a failed circulator pump.

Banging or gurgling pipes

Air in the system or low water level in a steam boiler.

Pressure too high or low

A failed expansion tank, leaking relief valve or a slow leak in the loop.

Method.

How we diagnose a boiler

We start at the thermostat and work toward the burner. That means checking the call for heat, the control board, the circulator, the zone valves and the safety devices. We read the gauge, check the expansion tank and bleed air where needed.

If a component has failed we tell you which one, what it controls and whether a repair or replacement is wiser. We do not swap parts on a guess.

According to the 2021 International Mechanical Code (IMC) that Connecticut adopts, Chapter 10 covers boilers and pressure vessels, including relief valves and safety controls. We check each one at every visit and never bypass a safety device to get heat back.

Types.

Hot-water boilers and steam boilers fail in different ways

Hot-water boilers push heated water through baseboard or radiators with a pump. Steam boilers boil water and send steam up through pipes. Each has its own weak points.

Hot-water boiler

  • Circulator pump failure.
  • Zone valve sticking.
  • Air in the loop.
  • Expansion tank waterlogged.

Steam boiler

  • Low-water cutoff problems.
  • Stuck or clogged steam vents.
  • Hammering from pitched pipes.
  • Sediment in the boiler.
Decision.

Repair or replace a boiler

An older boiler can be worth repairing if the heat exchanger is sound and the part is common. Leaks from the boiler body, heavy corrosion or repeated lockouts usually point to replacement.

We give you the cost of the repair and, if it is a close call, the cost of replacing it so you can compare. If the heater and boiler share a flue, ask us to check the water heater at the same visit.

Common Questions.

Boiler repair questions in Windham

Do you handle boiler emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. Our line is answered 24/7 and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Tell us whether the boiler has power, what the gauge reads and which zones are cold.

Why is my boiler making banging noises?

It is often trapped air or low water. In steam systems it can also be wet steam hammering through pitched pipes. We bleed the air, check the water level and look at the pipe slope.

Why do some rooms stay cold while others are warm?

A stuck zone valve, air in the loop or a weak circulator are the usual suspects. We test each zone to find which one is not responding.

Can a leaking boiler be repaired?

A leak from a fitting or valve can be fixed. A leak from the boiler body means the unit is worn out and needs to be replaced.

What pressure should my boiler gauge show?

Most residential hot-water boilers sit in a modest range when cold, and your unit has a label. If the gauge is far outside that range, call us rather than adjusting it yourself.

Do you service oil, gas and propane boilers?

We service common residential boilers. Tell us the fuel and brand on the call so we can confirm we can help.

How can I avoid a boiler failure in winter?

Have it checked before the cold season, keep the area around it clear and watch for slow pressure loss or new noises. Early service is cheaper than an emergency call.
