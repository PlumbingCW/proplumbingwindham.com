Overview.

When repiping makes sense

Repiping is a big job, so it should not be the first answer to a leak. It makes sense when the pipes are failing across the house, when the water runs brown or when pressure has dropped because the inside of the pipe has narrowed.

If the leaks are isolated, a repair is cheaper. We show you what we see and explain whether the pipe is near the end of its life or simply has one bad spot.

Signs.

Signs your pipes are wearing out

Pipes fail in a pattern. These are the signs we look for.

Repeated leaks

New leaks appear after each repair, a sign that the pipe is weak throughout.

Discolored water

Rust from galvanized pipes shows as brown or orange water.

Low pressure at several fixtures

The inside of the pipe has narrowed from corrosion.

Pinholes in copper

Small leaks that show as green stains or damp patches.

Old pipe.

Galvanized and older copper pipe

Older homes in Willimantic and across eastern Connecticut may have galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside, or older copper that has aged. We do not assume. We check with a visual inspection and flow test.

Houses of different ages mix pipe types after past repairs, and mixed metals can cause their own corrosion at the joints. We note every material we find so you know what the house really has.

Materials.

PEX or copper

Both work well, and the right one depends on the house, the budget and your preferences.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) lists approved potable water piping, including copper and PEX, and sets how each is joined and supported. Both options here are on that list, and we pressure test the new pipe before any wall is closed.

PEX

  • Flexible, fewer fittings.
  • Faster to install in walls.
  • Resists freezing damage better.
  • Not for outdoor sun exposure.

Copper

  • Long track record.
  • Rigid and tidy in open basements.
  • Resists UV and heat.
  • Costs more in materials and labor.
The job.

What a repipe looks like for your household

We map the fixtures and plan the routes before cutting. Where we can, we run new pipe through basements and crawlspaces and keep wall access small. We shut off water for part of the day, and we give you a plan so you know when.

Drywall repair and paint are separate. We cut as little as we can and tell you where openings will be so you can plan the finish work. For pressure and shutoff upgrades along the way, see our plumbing installation page.

Local.

Repiping older Windham homes

Much of the housing near Main Street and Prospect Hill dates to the Victorian era, and houses of that age are often updated in pieces. A repipe can be done in phases when the budget calls for it, starting with the worst section.

If you are in Coventry or Mansfield and the home is newer, repiping is rare. A leak there is more likely a single failed fitting, and a leak detection visit is the better first step. See our leak detection page.

Common Questions.

Repiping questions in Windham

How do I know if I need to repipe?

Repeated leaks, discolored water, low pressure and visible corrosion are the signs. We inspect and tell you if repairs are enough.

How long does a repipe take?

It depends on the size of the home. We give a realistic schedule once we see the house.

Will you have to open all my walls?

We cut as few openings as we can. Many runs go through basements and crawlspaces.

Is PEX safe for drinking water?

Yes. PEX approved for potable water is used widely in homes.

Can I repipe in phases?

Yes. We can start with the worst section and plan the rest.

Do I need a permit to repipe?

Often yes. We ask about your address and tell you what applies.
