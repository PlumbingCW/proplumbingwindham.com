A leaking or jammed disposal in the middle of dinner

A disposal that leaks under the sink or backs up with food is a kitchen emergency, and our 24/7 line handles those calls. While you wait, switch off the disposal at the wall switch and the breaker, and never put a hand inside the grinding chamber.

If water is leaking, place a bowl under the sink and close the cold-water shutoff valves under the cabinet. Keep the dishwasher off, because it drains through the disposal.

Symptoms.

What the symptom tells us

Disposals fail in a handful of ways, and the sound or sign usually points straight to the cause.

Humming but not grinding

The motor has power but the flywheel is jammed.

Nothing at all when switched on

A tripped overload, a failed switch or a dead motor.

Leak from the bottom

A cracked housing or failed seal, which means replacement.

Leak at the top or sides

A loose flange or drain connection, which is often repairable.

Slow draining or bad smell

A clog in the trap or branch line, not always the disposal.

Jams.

Jam clearing and resets

A jam is the most common problem and often the easiest. With the power off, we use a wrench on the bottom of the unit to free the flywheel. We also check for the object that caused the jam, such as a utensil, bone or cap.

Many disposals have a reset button on the bottom. If it trips again after being reset, the motor may be failing and a replacement is the better path.

Decision.

Repair or replacement: how we decide

A disposal under ten years old with a minor fault is worth fixing. One with a cracked housing, a seized motor or repeated overload trips is not. The cost of the repair, the age of the unit and the likelihood of a repeat problem decide.

We quote both options so you can choose, and we install a new unit to match your sink flange when we can. Details on code connections are in our plumbing installation page.

The 2020 National Electrical Code (NEC), adopted with the 2022 Connecticut State Building Code, governs the power connection, and the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) governs the trap and drain. We follow both and call in an electrician when the wiring needs more than a plug.

Drain line.

The drain behind the disposal

Many "disposal problems" are really drain problems. A disposal grinds food, but the pipe behind it still has to carry it away. Grease and ground food collect in the trap and the branch line and cause backups.

If the sink drains slowly even with a working disposal, we clear the line behind it. Our drain cleaning service covers kitchen clogs in more detail.

Common Questions.

Garbage disposal questions from Windham homes

Do you fix garbage disposals at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Switch off the unit and the breaker before you call.

Why is my disposal humming but not spinning?

It is jammed. We free the flywheel with a wrench from the underside and look for the object that caused it.

Why is my garbage disposal leaking?

Leaks come from the top flange, the side connections or the housing. A flange or side connection is often fixable. A leak from the housing means the unit needs replacing.

What should never go down a disposal?

Grease, bones, fibrous vegetables like celery, coffee grounds in bulk and starchy food such as pasta and rice. They jam the unit or clog the drain behind it.

Can I put my hand in to clear a jam?

Never. Switch off the power at the breaker and use a wrench on the underside or a tool, not your hand.

Do you install new disposals?

Yes. We swap in a new unit and reconnect the drain and power, and we check the drain line while we are there.
