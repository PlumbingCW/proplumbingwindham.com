Hydro jetting when a backup will not wait

A main line that is blocked and backing up into the house is an emergency, and we handle it at any hour. If water is rising, stop using sinks, tubs, the washer and toilets, and keep people out of the lowest floor.

Jetting is the follow-up when a cable did not hold. It uses water at high pressure to clean the full pipe wall, which is why it is the better choice for grease, scale and roots.

Process.

How hydro jetting actually works

A hose with a special nozzle goes into the drain through a cleanout. The nozzle sends jets of water backward to clean the wall and forward to pull the hose along. As it moves, it scours scale, grease and debris off the pipe and carries it downstream.

We start with a camera if the line is unknown. That tells us the pipe material, the pipe condition and whether jetting is safe, because a cracked or collapsed pipe should not be jetted.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) calls for cleanouts on building drains and sewers, and they are what makes jetting practical. If your line lacks one, we explain where one would go before we start.

Comparison.

Cable versus jetter: when the extra step is worth it

A cable is quick and works for most single clogs. A jetter costs more and takes more equipment, so it earns its keep where a cable keeps failing.

Cable or snake

  • Fast for a soft clog.
  • Leaves a path through the blockage.
  • Can leave grease on the wall.
  • Good first step.

Hydro jetting

  • Cleans the full pipe wall.
  • Handles scale and grease.
  • Cuts and flushes roots.
  • Needs a sound pipe.

What it clears.

Grease, scale and roots: the three jobs jetting does well

Grease lines are common in kitchens and restaurants, where fat cools in the pipe and builds a solid layer that narrows the drain. Jetting cuts and flushes it away.

Scale builds in older iron lines and narrows the opening so toilets and sinks slow down together. Roots enter at joints in older clay or iron lines and grow into a dense mat. A jetter with a root-cutting head clears them, but roots grow back, and we tell you what to expect.

Limits.

When jetting is not the right tool

Jetting is not for every pipe. A collapsed, offset or badly cracked line can be damaged by high pressure, and a camera inspection comes first when we are not sure. Very thin or old pipes may need a gentler method.

If the camera shows a break, we explain repair options before any cleaning. Our sewer line repair service covers that route, and the camera inspection page explains what we look for.

Common Questions.

Hydro jetting questions in Windham

Do you offer hydro jetting emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. A blocked main line is treated as urgent.

Is hydro jetting safe for old pipes?

It depends on the condition. We use a camera when the pipe is old or unknown and skip jetting if the line is cracked or collapsed.

How is jetting different from snaking?

A snake pokes a hole through a clog. A jetter cleans the whole pipe wall with high-pressure water, which removes buildup a cable leaves behind.

Will jetting stop tree roots for good?

No. It clears them, but roots can return if the pipe is open at a joint. We talk about repair options if they keep coming back.

How long does a jetting visit take?

Many jobs finish within a couple of hours, though long or heavily blocked lines can take longer. We give a realistic window on the call.

Do I need a cleanout for jetting?

A cleanout helps a lot. If you do not have one, we can often access the line through a toilet or other opening, and we can talk about adding one.
