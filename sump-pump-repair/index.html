Sump pump failed and water is rising

If water is climbing in the pit and the pump is silent, act fast. Check that it is plugged in and the breaker has not tripped. Unplug the pump before touching it, and if the water is above the pump and near outlets, keep clear and call us.

Our 24/7 line is open for storm calls. In a heavy rain, a bucket or a wet vac can slow the rise while we are on the way.

Failures.

Why sump pumps fail

Most failures come from a few causes, and a quick check can often tell which.

Pump runs but does not move water

A stuck check valve, clogged intake or worn impeller.

Pump will not turn on

A failed float switch or no power.

Pump cycles constantly

A stuck float or a check valve failing and letting water drain back.

Loud or grinding noise

Debris in the pit or a worn motor bearing.

Options.

Repair, replace or add a backup

A pump that is several years old with a worn float switch is often worth a switch swap. One with a failing motor is better replaced, and we size the new pump to your pit and your water volume.

A backup pump with a battery or a water-powered unit keeps the basement dry during a power outage, which is when storms often cut power. We install either and explain how to test it.

Install.

Installing a new sump pump and pit

A new installation starts with a pit at the lowest point of the basement. We cut the floor, set a liner, fit the pump and run a discharge line to a safe place away from the foundation.

The discharge matters. A line that empties near the house can send the water straight back, and a line that freezes can stop the pump. We route it to drain away and advise on freeze protection. This is related to the work on our waterproofing page.

Chapter 11 of the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) covers storm drainage, including sump pits and pumping systems. Sump discharge should go to a storm or surface outlet and not into a sanitary sewer or a septic system, and we plan it that way.

Upkeep.

Maintenance that keeps a pump working

Pour a bucket of water into the pit twice a year to confirm the pump starts and drains. Clean the intake screen, check that the discharge line is clear and test the backup battery.

We can do this as part of a visit. A quick test before the wet season is much cheaper than a flooded basement.

Local.

Sump pumps in eastern Connecticut basements

Many homes in the Natchaug and Willimantic river areas, as well as in Columbia and Coventry near the lakes, sit in places where the water table rises in wet seasons. A basement that is dry in August can see water after a hard rain in spring.

If your pump runs constantly, a failing check valve or a high water table may be the cause, and we tell you which.

Common Questions.

Sump pump questions in Windham

Do you handle sump pump emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Tell us how high the water is in the pit.

How long do sump pumps last?

They vary by use, and a pump that runs often wears faster. We check yours and tell you where it stands.

Do I need a battery backup?

If the power goes out in storms and your basement can flood, yes, it is worth considering.

Why does my sump pump run all the time?

The water table may be high, a check valve may be failing or the float may be stuck. We find the cause.

Where should the discharge go?

Away from the foundation, downhill and in a way that will not freeze shut.

Can you install a pump in a basement without a pit?

Yes. We can cut and set a pit and run a discharge, and we explain what the work involves.
