The area.

Worker neighborhoods across the river

The town plan calls this part of Willimantic the Over-the-River or South Side. It is mostly residential, with small commercial and office uses and some undeveloped land. The plan lists Jillson Hill, The Oaks, Quercus Avenue, Crescent Street and Fairview Avenue as historic neighborhoods built as model worker housing for the American Thread Company.

The Oaks has its own record. A local history archive says the Willimantic Linen Company developed it in 1890 as part of a housing program that began in 1865, with forty houses built from four floor plans. Jillson Hill takes its name from a mill village the Jillson brothers built in the 1820s.

The plan says the south side is only partially served by public water and sewer, so we do not assume your street is. When you call, tell us whether you pay a water and sewer bill. That tells us whether we are working on a municipal line or on a private system.

It also changes what a camera inspection can find and what a repair involves. If your street is on the sewer, our sewer camera inspection shows the building line up to the connection.

Common calls.

Fixtures, drains and water heaters in compact houses

Smaller houses mean the same pipes serve more of the building. A kitchen sink, a washer and a bathroom may share one drain run, so a partial blockage shows up in several places at once. A drain cleaning visit clears the shared line.

In tight utility spaces, a water heater has less room to work around. Our water heater installation page explains how we fit a unit to the space. For a drip or a running toilet, see faucet repair and toilet repair.
