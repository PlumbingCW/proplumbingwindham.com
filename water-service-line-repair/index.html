A broken water line is a same-day problem

If water is bubbling up in the yard, the meter is spinning with every fixture off or the whole house has lost pressure, the service line may have failed. Shut off the main valve inside the house, and call our 24/7 line.

If you cannot find your main valve, tell us. Some older homes have a curb stop or shutoff in a pit near the street that only the utility can operate, and we help you work out who to call.

Basics.

What a service line is and where it can fail

The service line carries water from the street main, or from a private well, to your house. It enters through the foundation, passes the meter if you are on public water and feeds the whole plumbing system.

Failures happen at joints, at the entry point through the foundation and along the buried run. Age, frost movement and roots all play a part, and older materials such as galvanized or lead-era lines tend to corrode.

Signs.

Signs the line is leaking or failing

Buried leaks rarely announce themselves, but they leave clues.

Soft or wet spot in the yard

Water is rising above the pipe and saturating the soil.

Sudden drop in pressure

A large leak reduces flow to the house.

Meter moves with everything off

Water is leaving the system somewhere between the meter and the house.

Murky water after pressure changes

A break lets soil into the line.

Pressure.

Low water pressure: line, valve or fixture?

Low pressure is not always a broken line. A partly closed valve, a clogged filter or a failing pressure-reducing valve can have the same effect. In rural homes, a pressure tank or pump on a private well is another place to look.

We check pressure at an outdoor tap, test the main valve and compare hot and cold. If the pressure is low only at one fixture, the cause is local to that fixture.

Options.

Repair or replace the line

A short break can be repaired with a coupling. A line that has failed more than once, or is made of old material, is usually better replaced. Replacing means a trench, though trenchless methods are sometimes possible.

We explain which fits your line, walk the route with you and tell you who handles what, since some work near the street may involve the town. Our burst pipe repair page covers failures inside the house.

Material, depth and permit rules for water service lines come from the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and from the town and the water provider. We confirm what applies before any trench is opened.

Local.

Service lines across Windham, Lebanon and Columbia

Homes on public water in Willimantic have a meter and a shutoff, while many rural homes in Lebanon and Columbia are on private wells with a line from the well to a pressure tank. The repair depends on which you have, so we ask at the start.

Frost depth matters in this part of Connecticut. A line that sits too shallow can crack in a hard winter, and we advise on depth when we replace one.

Common Questions.

Water service line questions in Windham

Do you repair water lines at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. The 24/7 line is answered around the clock, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Shut off the main first if you can.

How do you find a buried water line leak?

We use a pipe locator, listening gear and pressure tests to narrow down the spot before digging.

Will you have to dig up my yard?

A repair needs at least a small trench. We explain the route and what the surface will look like before work starts.

Who is responsible for the water line?

The part on your property is generally yours. The part near the street may belong to the utility or town. We help sort out which is which.

My well water pressure dropped, is that the line?

It could be the line, the pressure tank, the pump or a clogged filter. We check each, and a well-pump failure may call for a well specialist.

What materials do you use for replacement lines?

We use pipe suitable for buried drinking water service, and we choose between copper and PEX or similar based on conditions and code.
