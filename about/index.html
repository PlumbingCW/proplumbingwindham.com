About Windham Pro Plumbing

A Willimantic plumbing company with a 24/7 line, a clear price before work and plain explanations.

Written by the Windham Pro Plumbing team. Updated November 14, 2026.

Who we are

Windham Pro Plumbing is a plumbing company based at 967 Main St in Willimantic, Connecticut. We are licensed and insured, and we serve homes and small businesses in Windham and four nearby towns.

We built the service around one idea: a plumbing problem should be easy to report and easy to understand. One number answers around the clock, and we explain what we find in plain terms.

How we work

Every job starts with a call. We ask a few questions, tell you what to shut off and send a technician. You get a clear price before work starts, and you decide whether to go ahead.

When a problem is not ours to fix, we say so. We do not pump septic tanks, repair foundations or do roofing, and we tell you which trade to call instead.

What we do not do

We do not publish fake reviews, invented numbers or promises we cannot keep. We do not guarantee an arrival time, because weather, traffic and other emergencies change it. We tell you what we can do and when.

According to the Connecticut Department of Consumer Protection, a plumber's license status can be checked in its online lookup, and our work follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) the state adopts. We encourage you to use it for any contractor.

How a call works

A person answers the line at any hour. We ask what is happening, where the water is coming from and whether anything is unsafe, such as a gas smell or water near electrical outlets.

Then we tell you what to shut off, such as the main water valve or the breaker for a wet room. We give you a plain description of the likely cause and a clear price before we start any work.

A technician arrives, confirms the problem and explains the options. If the fix is simple, we do it. If it needs a bigger plan, we explain the choices and let you decide.

What we cover

Our work spans water heaters, boilers, drains, sewer lines, leaks, burst pipes, toilets, faucets, sump pumps and planned installs. You can see each one on the services page, with notes on how we diagnose and what changes the cost.

We also write guides for homeowners on our plumbing blog, covering questions we hear often, from fill valves to sump pumps. They are meant to help you decide what you can do yourself and when to call.

How we think about pricing

Price depends on the problem, the access, the parts and the time of day. We name those factors on every service page so you can see what drives a quote. We do not quote over the phone when the cause is unknown, because a wrong number helps no one.

Insurance may cover sudden damage such as a burst pipe, but usually not wear or gradual leaks. We can describe a failure in plain terms for a claim, and we suggest you call your insurer early.

What to have ready when you call

It helps to know your street address and ZIP code, where the main water shutoff is and whether the house is on public water or a private well. For a water heater call, the brand and fuel type save time. For a drain call, tell us which fixtures are affected.

If you rent, tell us whether the landlord has authorized the repair. We work with landlords and tenants, and we document what we find for both.

Respect for your home

A plumbing call happens in someone's kitchen, basement or bathroom. We put down floor protection, keep the work area tidy and clean up before we leave. We explain what we found in plain words and show you the failed part where we can.

Safety comes first around gas and electricity. If we find a gas smell or a wet electrical hazard, we stop work and tell you what to do before anything else.

Five checks for choosing any plumber

Ask for the contractor's license number and look it up on the state lookup. Ask for a written scope and price before work begins. Ask what happens if the job turns out different from the plan, and who decides.

Ask whether the work needs a permit and who pulls it. Ask how the repair will be tested and what the warranty covers in writing. A contractor who welcomes these questions is usually one worth hiring, and we welcome them too.
