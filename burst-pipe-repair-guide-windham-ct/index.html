Burst Pipes Repair for Windham, CT Homes

A burst pipe is a short, stressful emergency that you handle with a few clear steps. This guide covers what to do first, how repairs work and how to prevent the next one.

Written by the Windham Pro Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Act fast.

The first five minutes

Close the main water valve. Find it now if you do not know where it is, because you will not want to search in a flood. Then open the lowest faucet to drain the line and switch off power to wet areas.

Move valuables off the floor, take photos of the damage for your insurer and call a plumber. If the ceiling is sagging with water, stay out from under it.

Causes.

Why pipes burst

Freezing is the usual cause. Ice blocks the pipe, pressure builds between the blockage and a closed tap and the pipe splits where it is weakest. The split is often not where the ice is, but downstream.

Other causes include corrosion, high water pressure, a loose fitting and impact damage. In older homes, years of small repairs can leave weak spots.

Frozen supply in exterior walls

Cold air reaches the pipe and ice forms.

Unheated spaces

Garages, crawlspaces and attics are common freeze points.

Old or corroded pipe

Thin walls fail under normal pressure.

High pressure

Pressure above the normal range stresses joints and fittings.

Windham note.

Know your shutoff before winter

Connecticut's winters put pipes in exterior walls and unheated spaces at risk. If you are on public water, there is a meter and a shutoff where the line enters your home. If you are on a private well, the pressure tank and the pump breaker are also part of the shutoff plan.

Walk through your house and tag the valves before the first hard freeze. Our burst pipe repair page explains what we do when you call.

According to Connecticut Department of Public Health guidance, well water should be retested after repairs to the pump or pipes.

Repair.

How repairs work

Repair starts with shutting off water and locating every break. Then we cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings suited to the material. We pressure test before closing walls.

Aftermath.

Drying out and insurance

Water damage gets worse fast. Mold can start within a couple of days, so remove wet items, run fans and dehumidifiers and open cabinets. Call your insurer early and keep receipts for drying and repairs.

Insurers often cover sudden bursts, but not damage from a long-neglected problem. Photograph everything before cleanup. A plumber can describe the failure in plain terms for your claim, and we explain our approach on the leak detection page.

Prevention.

Preventing the next burst

Keep heat on, even in a house you leave for a stretch. Open cabinet doors on exterior walls during cold snaps. Let a trickle run at the farthest faucet when temperatures plunge. Insulate pipes in unheated areas and seal air leaks near pipes.

Disconnect garden hoses and shut off exterior spigots. If a pipe has frozen once, it will probably freeze again. We can check and protect those spots at a visit.

Pitfalls.

Mistakes that make a burst worse

The most common mistake is waiting. A pipe that is still feeding water keeps flooding until the main is shut off, so shut it off first and think second. Another is using electrical appliances near water, which is dangerous.

People also forget to turn off the water heater when the water is shut off, which can damage the tank if it runs dry on an electric unit. Switch off power to an electric heater at the breaker, and set a gas heater to pilot or off.

Checklist.

A simple winter checklist for your house

A short list done in the fall prevents most freeze calls. Insulate pipes in garages, crawlspaces and attics. Seal gaps where pipes enter exterior walls. Drain and shut off outdoor spigots and store hoses.

Know where the main shutoff is and make sure it turns. Set a reminder to check the thermostat if you travel, and ask a neighbor to look in if the power goes out while you are gone.

Next step.

When to call and what to tell us

Call as soon as the water is shut off. Tell us where the water is coming from, what the pipe is made of if you know and whether the ceiling or floor is wet. If you are in Columbia or Lebanon on a well, tell us that too.

We answer at any hour and send a technician the same day when one is available.

Common Questions.

Windham burst pipe questions

What do I do first when a pipe bursts?

Close the main water valve, open the lowest faucet, switch off power to wet areas and call a plumber.

Will my insurance cover a burst pipe in Windham?

Often for sudden bursts, but not for neglect. Call your insurer promptly and keep photos and receipts.

How do I find the main valve?

Look near where the water line enters the house, often in the basement near the front wall or by the water heater.

Can a frozen pipe be thawed without calling a plumber?

Sometimes. Open the faucet and warm the pipe gently. If there is any crack or water, shut off the main and call.

How quickly should I dry out the house?

Start right away. Mold can begin within a couple of days in wet materials.
