Housing eras.

A town built in layers: colonial farms, mid-century homes and subdivisions

Coventry has some of the oldest houses in the area, including farmhouses from the 1700s and 1800s. It also has capes and ranches from the mid-century years and newer subdivisions. The Nathan Hale Homestead is one landmark from the town's early days.

Each era has its own plumbing habits. We ask when the house was built, because that tells us what pipes and fixtures to expect before we arrive.

Old systems.

Old pipes, old drains and what we find behind the walls

In the oldest houses, a plumbing repair often uncovers decades of changes. A galvanized line might join to copper at an odd point, and a drain might change material in the middle of a run.

We tell you what we find and what it means. Where the system is mixed, we mark the points that are likely to fail next. Our leak detection page covers how we trace a leak without opening more wall than we need.

Connecticut's 2022 State Building Code adopts the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), so repairs in an old house follow current rules even when the rest of the pipe is not. We tell you where new work meets old and what that junction needs.

Before we arrive.

If a pipe bursts or a leak starts in Coventry

A leak in an old house can move through plaster, floors and ceilings before it shows. These steps limit the damage as soon as you notice a problem.

Close the main valve

Shut off the water where it enters the house. If it is stuck, call us, do not force it.

Switch off power to wet areas

Turn off the breakers and stay clear of wet outlets and appliances.

Move belongings

Lift items off the floor and cover furniture. Plaster ceilings can drop when soaked.

Sewer and septic.

Sewer areas, septic areas and the town's role

Parts of Coventry are served by a public sewer system managed by the town's Water Pollution Control Authority, while other parts rely on private septic systems. We ask which you have, since a backup on a public line may involve a different fix than one on a septic system.

We clear and inspect the line from the house. If the issue is in the main street line, we tell you to contact the town. Our sewer line repair page explains the options when the pipe is damaged.

Lake and roads.

Lake homes, hills and Route 31 and Route 44

Coventry Lake brings water, higher water tables and basement dampness to nearby homes. Hillier parts of town have wells and long driveways. We reach most addresses along Route 31 and Route 44 from our Willimantic base.

Basements near the lake can seep after heavy rain, and a failing sump is a frequent call. See our sump pump page. If you are near the Mansfield line, our Mansfield page covers the Storrs side.

Heat and hot water.

Water heaters and boilers in Coventry homes

Older Coventry houses often have boilers for heat and tank heaters for hot water, and some have both fed from the same flue. A failure in one can affect the other. We check the venting when we repair either.

Our boiler repair page covers heating calls, and we handle the water heater at the same visit when it makes sense.
