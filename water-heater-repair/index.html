Water heater leaking or no hot water right now

A water heater that leaks or quits is an emergency plumbing problem, and our 24/7 line is open for exactly that call. While you wait, switch the heater off at the breaker or turn the gas control to pilot or off, then close the cold-water valve on top of the tank.

If water is spreading across the floor, keep everyone away from outlets and the heater itself. Do not try to plug a leaking tank, because the pressure inside makes that unsafe.

Diagnosis.

Four symptoms and what each one usually means

Most repair calls start with something you can hear, see or feel. Matching your symptom to the likely cause helps us arrive with the right parts on the truck.

Water pooling at the base

Often a loose fitting, a weeping relief valve or a drain valve. A seam leak in the tank itself means the heater is finished.

No hot water at all

On electric units this is usually a failed element, thermostat or tripped limit switch. On gas it is often the thermocouple, gas valve or igniter.

Popping or rumbling

Sediment baked onto the bottom of the tank. Flushing can help early on, but a heavily scaled tank runs hot and wears out fast.

Rusty hot water

If only the hot side runs rusty, the tank lining or anode rod is failing. Rust on both sides points to the pipes instead.

Method.

How we find the fault before replacing anything

We check the simple things first because they are often the whole answer. For electric heaters that means testing each element and thermostat with a meter. For gas heaters it means checking the pilot flame, thermocouple, burner and flue draft.

We also look at the temperature and pressure relief valve, its discharge pipe, the anode rod and the shutoff valves. A cheap part that is replaced on the wrong guess costs you twice, so we confirm the failure first.

The 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), which Connecticut adopts through its 2022 State Building Code, requires a temperature and pressure relief valve and a discharge pipe on a tank water heater. We test both on every repair, because a blocked or missing discharge line is a safety fault and not a detail.

Leaks.

Why a leaking tank ends the repair conversation

Water heater leaks are among the most common reasons people call us, and they split into two groups. A leak from a fitting, valve or connection is a repair. A leak from the steel tank is not, because the lining has failed and no patch holds.

We find the source by drying the tank, wrapping the connections in dry paper towel and watching where the first drop appears. If it is the tank, we say so quickly so you can move on to a replacement heater the same day.

Gas safety.

What a gas heater needs that an electric one does not

Gas models add a second set of failure points. The burner can clog, the flue can draw poorly and the gas valve can fail. Any smell of gas means leaving the house and calling the gas utility or 911 before you call us.

We check the combustion air, the draft hood and the vent connection as part of every gas repair. If the heater shares a flue with a furnace or a boiler, we look at that too, because a weak draft affects both.

Many homes in Willimantic and Windham Center have heaters in basements with narrow stairs, low ceilings and a single floor drain. A heater that fails there can run for hours before anyone notices, so we ask about the drain and the stairs when you call.

Common Questions.

Water heater repair questions from Windham homeowners

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Windham?

Yes. Our dispatch line is answered 24/7, and we send a technician the same day when one is available. Tell us whether the tank is leaking, because that changes what we ask you to shut off first.

Why is my water heater leaking from the bottom?

Water at the base comes from a loose fitting, the relief valve, the drain valve or the tank itself. If it is the tank, the heater needs replacing. We trace the source before recommending anything.

Can a water heater with no hot water be fixed the same day?

Often yes. Electric units usually need an element or thermostat and gas units a thermocouple or gas valve. If the part is not on the truck, we tell you the plan before we leave.

How old is too old to repair a water heater?

There is no single number. A well-kept tank can run past 12 years, while a scaled tank in hard-use may fail sooner. We weigh the age, the leak history and the part cost together.

Is it safe to keep using a water heater that is dripping?

Not for long. A drip from a fitting may be minor, but a drip from the tank body will get worse. Shut off the power or gas and the cold-water valve, then call.

Do you repair gas and electric heaters both?

Yes. We work on gas, electric and oil-fired tank heaters, plus the venting and relief valves attached to them. If we find a gas leak risk, we stop and advise you on the safe next step.

What should I do while I wait for the plumber?

Turn off the power or gas, close the cold-water supply at the top of the tank and clear anything valuable from the floor. Do not touch wet electrical outlets.
