The area.

A village on the Natchaug River and the US 6 corridor

North Windham is centered near the crossing of Routes 6, 66 and 203. The Natchaug River falls here, near the point where Windham, Chaplin and Mansfield meet. Small cotton, woolen and paper mills grew up around the falls in the 1800s.

Today the town plan describes commercial and light manufacturing use along Routes 6 and 66, with low-density residential and farm land away from those roads. Windham Airport and the Windham Club and Golf Course are also here.

Two kinds of customer

Shop owners and homeowners call about very different fixtures, so we ask early which one you are.

A clear edge is hard to draw

The town plan says the line between North Windham and Windham Center is not particularly clear.

Utility service varies

The plan does not map water and sewer service for this village, so confirm yours with the town or utility.

Close to Mansfield and Chaplin

Homes.

Homes along the quieter streets

Away from the strip, North Windham is mostly lower-density housing. Lots are often larger than in Willimantic, and the mix runs from older farmhouses to newer homes. We never guess what a house has based on where it sits, so the first question we ask is whether you are on public water and sewer or on a well and septic system.

That answer changes the diagnosis. Low pressure on a well points us to the pressure tank and pump. Low pressure on a public line points us to the shutoffs and fixtures. See our water heater repair page and drain cleaning page for the usual home calls.

Business.

Shops, restaurants and offices on the corridor

Businesses on US 6 and Route 66 have their own plumbing needs, such as restroom fixtures, floor drains, grease-prone kitchen lines and storage water heaters. We plan visits around opening hours when the problem allows. If water is spreading, we treat it as the first priority.

Our commercial plumbing page covers how we work with small businesses. For a restaurant with a slow main, a hydro jetting visit may clear the line better than a cable can.

Before we arrive.

If water is spreading before we arrive

Whether it is a house or a shop, the first minutes matter. These steps help in either case.

Close the main

Shut the main water valve fully. In a shop, ask the landlord where it is if you do not know.

Cut power near water

Turn off breakers for wet areas and stay out of standing water around outlets.

Keep customers and kids clear

Rope off or close wet floors to avoid falls.

Call and give details

Tell us the address, what is leaking and what you have already shut off.
