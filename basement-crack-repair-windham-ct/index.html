Basement Waterproofing: Crack Repair for Windham, CT Homes

Not every basement crack is the same. Some are cosmetic, some leak and some signal movement. This guide helps you tell which is which and what the repair looks like.

Written by the Windham Pro Plumbing team. Published November 14, 2026.

Overview.

Reading a basement crack

The shape, width and direction of a crack tell you a lot. Hairline cracks in poured concrete walls are common and often come from shrinkage as the concrete cured. They may or may not leak.

Wider cracks, cracks that are stepped along block joints or horizontal cracks that bow inward point to pressure or movement. Those are a structural concern, not a sealing job.

Types.

Types of cracks and what they mean

Use this as a rough guide, not a diagnosis.

Vertical hairline

Often shrinkage. Usually sealable if it leaks.

Diagonal crack near a corner or window

Settlement or stress. Watch for widening.

Stair-step crack in block

Movement along mortar joints. Have a structural professional look.

Horizontal crack with inward bow

Soil pressure on the wall. A structural issue.

Limits.

What a seal can and cannot do

A flexible injection seals a crack from the inside by filling it with material that bonds and stays flexible. That works for a stable crack that leaks. It does not stop the water from reaching the wall. It only blocks its path.

If the crack moves or the wall is bowing, sealing is a patch over a problem. A structural professional should evaluate it, and we say so when we see signs.

Sealing can help when

  • The crack is stable and narrow.
  • Water seeps but the wall is straight.
  • There is no sign of movement.

Sealing will not help when

  • The wall is bowing.
  • The crack is widening over time.
  • The floor is pushing up from pressure.
Windham note.

Call 811 before any digging outside

Connecticut participates in the national 811 Call Before You Dig system. If a basement repair plan includes digging outside, such as exterior excavation or a new discharge line, call 811 first so utilities can mark buried lines.

Interior crack repair does not need digging, but drainage and sump work often does. Our sump pump service covers the interior drainage side.

According to the 811 program, calling before you dig gets buried utility lines marked at no charge.

Rule out plumbing.

Water that is not from the crack

A wet wall is not always groundwater. A leaking supply pipe, a failed drain or condensation on cold surfaces can all look like seepage. Before you pay for a crack repair, rule out plumbing.

We check the supply lines, the drains and the water meter. If the meter moves with the house shut off, the water is a leak, not seepage. Our leak detection page explains how.

Drainage.

Interior drainage and the sump

If water enters at several points or at the wall-floor joint, an interior drain along the wall may lead water to a sump pit. That system works with a pump and a discharge line, and it is a plumbing job.

Exterior fixes, such as grading, gutters and downspouts, are cheaper than many people expect and should come first. Keeping roof water away from the foundation reduces the load on any interior system.

Monitor.

Track a crack before you decide

A simple way to learn whether a crack is moving is to mark it. Draw a pencil line across the crack at both ends with a date, and photograph it with a ruler beside it. Check it every few weeks and after heavy rain.

If the ends grow past the marks or the width changes, write it down and talk to a structural professional. A crack that stays still for a year is usually a sealing job, and one that moves is a different problem.

Outside first.

Gutters, grading and the cheap fixes first

Before paying for any interior work, look outside. Gutters that overflow, downspouts that dump next to the foundation and soil that slopes toward the house all push water toward the basement wall. Extending downspouts several feet away and regrading the soil near the wall is cheap compared to most interior systems.

Check after the next heavy rain. If the damp patch shrinks or disappears, the cause was surface water, and the crack is no longer the main issue.

Condensation.

Humidity that looks like a leak

A basement can feel damp with no water coming through the wall. Warm humid air meets cold concrete and condenses, leaving wet patches and a musty smell, especially in summer. Taping a square of plastic to the wall for a day tells you which side the moisture is on.

Water beneath the plastic means seepage from outside. Water on the room side means condensation, and a dehumidifier and better ventilation will do more than any crack seal.

Plan.

Planning the repair

Start by finding out when the water appears. Seasonal, rain-triggered water points to groundwater and drainage. Water that appears with no rain points to plumbing. Note the spots, take photos after rain and keep track of how a crack changes.

We help with the plumbing and drainage side in Windham and Mansfield, and we recommend foundation or structural help when we see movement.

Common Questions.

Windham basement crack questions

Is every basement crack a problem?

No. Many hairline cracks are cosmetic. Wide, stepped or bowing cracks need a professional look.

Can a crack be fixed from the inside?

A stable crack can be sealed from the inside. A moving one needs a structural fix.

Why does water come in only after heavy rain?

That points to groundwater, grading or gutters. Drainage and exterior fixes often help.

Do you do foundation repair?

No. We handle plumbing and drainage, and we tell you when you need a structural specialist.
